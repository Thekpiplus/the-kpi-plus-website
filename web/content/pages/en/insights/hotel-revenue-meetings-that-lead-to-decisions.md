# How to run a revenue meeting that ends in a usable decision

A useful hotel revenue meeting does not stop at yesterday’s numbers. It helps the team see what changed, why it matters, and what to do next. When the meeting becomes a report-only ritual, pricing, channels, marketing and reservations can drift without a shared revenue and growth direction.

**Key points:** Confirm the forward revenue question · Review only signals that affect that question · Agree a decision, an owner and a review date · Note what to check before the next meeting

## A revenue meeting should end in a decision

The question to ask is not only “what happened?” but “given what happened, what do we need to decide?”

## Start with the signals that matter

Meetings are easier to run when the team uses a short list of signals instead of opening every report. The right list varies by hotel, but it should usually support discussion of demand pace, rate position, booking lead time, channel mix, cancellation context and direct-booking activity.

The goal is not to recreate a dashboard on a screen. It is to find the few signals that could change a choice. If a signal does not affect options, keep it in reference material rather than the main conversation.

## Tie every material change to a decision

When something important moves, the team should name the decision under review. Softer pace may raise questions about availability, rate conditions, campaign timing or reservation follow-up. Stronger demand may raise questions about inventory, channel openness or package readiness.

Plain language helps. “Review rates” is still vague. “Hold published weekend rates, reduce allotment to lower-value channels, and review direct-booking demand again in three days” is a decision the team can act on.

## Every next step needs an owner and a review point

Revenue and growth decisions work better with a named owner, a date and a signal for follow-up. Ownership does not mean one person carries the whole outcome. It means someone drives what was agreed and brings observations back to the team.

Review points stop the team treating any decision as permanent by default. Hotel demand changes. Decisions should be tied to a date, a market condition or a performance signal that tells the team when to look again.

## A meeting rhythm that works in practice

A simple rhythm is enough:

- Confirm the forward revenue and growth question
- Review only the signals that affect that question
- Agree the decision, owner and review date
- Record what to check before the next meeting

This keeps revenue management, distribution, paid demand, reservations and hotel leadership in the same conversation. The meeting is not the destination. It is the point where revenue, sales and marketing work are pointed in the same direction.

## Next step

Pick one upcoming meeting and try this structure. Start with one revenue or growth question, limit the signals, then name the decision, owner and review date in full. The difference between reporting and a decision that drives work becomes clearer quickly.

**Example:** A revenue team starts with booking pace, soft dates and inventory limits, then sets the revenue question that must be decided in the next meeting.
