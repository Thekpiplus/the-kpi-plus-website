# Hotel SEO: How to Get Your Hotel Website on Page One of Google (2026 Update)

**Hotel SEO** is the work that makes your hotel's website show up on Google when travellers search for a stay that matches what you offer. Independent hotels can genuinely reach page one for their own name, for specific searches about location or trip type, and on Google Maps. For broad searches like "hotels in Phuket", most of the space at the top belongs to Google and the OTAs.

This guide is for owners, GMs and marketing teams in Thailand who want their website to drive more direct bookings. The question we answer: **which searches should we target, which SEO work comes first, and how do we measure whether it brings bookings** — not just rankings that look good in a report.

**Key points:** Win searches for your hotel's name first · Fix technical basics so Google can reach every page · Build complete room, location and FAQ pages · Manage Google Maps alongside your website · Measure clicks that lead to bookings

## What changed in 2026: more frequent ranking updates, and AI that books

Google has released three core updates in 2026 — in March, May and November. Search Engine Journal reported that the May update began on 21 May and was the fourth confirmed ranking update of the year. Google describes these updates as designed to surface relevant, satisfying content.

![Timeline of 2026 changes affecting hotel SEO: core updates in March, May and November, and hotel booking through Google's AI Mode in the United States from 27 August](/images/blog/hotel-seo/en/what-changed.webp)

Search is also becoming conversational. Since 27 August 2026, Google has let users in the United States search, compare and book hotels in one conversation in AI Mode, and SiteMinder's Changing Traveller Report 2026 found OTAs have overtaken search engines as the top starting point for hotel searches.

**What this means for hotels:** shortcuts to push rankings become riskier with every update. What lasts is a website with accurate, complete information that matches every other channel — because Google and AI tools both rely on that information.

## Which searches can hotel SEO realistically win?

Before starting, split searches into four types. Each has different winners and needs different work.

![Four search types for hotel SEO: hotel-name searches, specific searches by location or trip type, local searches on Google Maps, and broad searches dominated by OTAs](/images/blog/hotel-seo/en/search-types.webp)

| Search type | Example | Usually won by | Your chance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hotel name | "[hotel name] Kata" | Your website or an OTA | Very high — you must win it |
| Specific | "family hotel with pool near Kata Beach" | The page that answers most precisely | High, with the right page |
| Local | "hotels near me", "hotel near the airport" | Google Maps | High, with a complete profile and reviews |
| Broad | "hotels in Phuket" | Google's hotel module and OTAs | Low — use ads or OTAs instead |

**Watch out:** if an SEO provider guarantees the top spot for "hotel + province", ask how, and whether they report bookings. Google itself says no one can guarantee rankings.

## 1. Win your hotel-name searches first

People searching your name are closest to booking. If an OTA ranks above your own website, you're paying commission on guests who already found you.

- Your homepage title should include your name and location, e.g. "[Hotel name] | Hotel near Kata Beach, Phuket"
- Keep name, address and phone identical across your website, Business Profile and OTAs
- Add a direct booking link to your Business Profile and enable free booking links through a supported booking engine

**Measure:** in Google Search Console, check click-through rate on your brand searches. A low CTR means OTA results or ads are taking those clicks.

## 2. Make sure Google can reach and understand every page

Great content doesn't help if Google can't reach the page or it loads slowly on mobile. Technical basics come before content work.

![Hotel SEO technical basics checklist: crawling (Search Console, sitemap, HTTPS) and understanding (mobile speed, hreflang for multiple languages, Hotel structured data)](/images/blog/hotel-seo/en/technical-basics.webp)

- Connect Google Search Console and submit a sitemap
- Check that every room page is indexed
- Use HTTPS and keep pages fast on mobile, especially room photos
- For multiple languages, give each its own URL and use hreflang
- Add Hotel structured data with your real name, address and amenities

The KPI Plus [hotel searchability check (in Thai)](/tools/hotel-searchability-check) gives a quick first view of what to fix on your website and Google listing.

## 3. Build pages that answer specific searches

Each page should answer one question completely rather than putting everything on the homepage. Hotel websites that rank for specific searches usually have these pages.

![Site map of the five page types hotel SEO needs: homepage, a page per room type, location and getting-here page, experiences and packages, and FAQ](/images/blog/hotel-seo/en/page-map.webp)

| Page | Answers searches like | Must include |
|---|---|---|
| Each room type | "family room for four" | Size, beds, occupancy, view, real photos |
| Location and getting here | "hotel near Kata Beach" | Real distances, airport transfer, map |
| Experiences | "quiet honeymoon hotel" | What you actually deliver, photos, reviews |
| FAQ | "check-in time", "is there parking" | Short, direct answers matching real policy |

Each page needs an easy-to-find rates or booking button — the goal of SEO is bookings, not visits.

## 4. Manage Google Maps alongside your website

For local searches, Google often shows the map before website results. A complete Business Profile with steady reviews is part of hotel SEO, not a separate project. Check your category, hotel details, amenities, photos and website link match your site.

## 5. Earn mentions from relevant sources

Links and mentions from other websites still help Google trust you — but they must come from relevant sources: your provincial hotel association, partner restaurants and activities, travel media, or bloggers who actually stayed.

**Watch out:** never buy links in bulk from unrelated sites. Google treats it as spam, and it can pull down the whole site after an update.

## 6. Measure hotel SEO in bookings, not just rankings

Rankings change daily and differ by the searcher's location. Report clicks from Search Console and bookings or revenue from organic search in Google Analytics or your booking engine.

![Four hotel SEO metrics: clicks from Google Search Console, click-through rate on hotel-name searches, bookings from organic search, and revenue per visit](/images/blog/hotel-seo/en/seo-metrics.webp)

**Numbers to watch:** Search Console clicks (brand vs non-brand) · brand-search CTR · bookings from organic search · revenue per visit

## A 90-day hotel SEO plan

Start with technical work, then the pages closest to booking, then outside mentions. This 90-day plan suits most independent hotels.

| Period | Main work |
|---|---|
| Weeks 1–2 | Connect Search Console, fix unindexed pages, check mobile speed |
| Weeks 3–6 | Rewrite titles and descriptions; rebuild room and location pages |
| Weeks 7–10 | Build the FAQ page; align Business Profile with the website |
| Weeks 11–13 | Reach out to local partners; first results review; plan next quarter |

![A 90-day hotel SEO plan in four phases: technical audit, room and location pages, FAQ and Business Profile, then local mentions and measurement](/images/blog/hotel-seo/en/plan-90-days.webp)

## Planning example: a 35-room hotel near Kata Beach

Imagine a 35-room hotel whose website gets 1,200 Google clicks a month, almost all from its own name — yet an OTA ranks first for that name, and all room types sit on a single page.

After splitting rooms into three pages, writing a "Phuket airport to Kata Beach" guide and fixing the homepage title, the hotel might target 300 non-brand clicks a month within six months. At a 1.5% booking rate, that's four or five extra commission-free bookings a month. This is a planning example, not a result from any client.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does hotel SEO take to show results?

Foundational fixes — indexing, page titles, room pages — usually show changes in Search Console within four to eight weeks. Ranking for competitive searches typically takes three to six months or more, depending on your site's starting point and local competition. Track progress monthly rather than waiting for a single result.

### Can our website rank on page one for 'hotels in Phuket'?

It's very hard for an independent hotel. For broad searches like this, Google usually shows its own hotel module and OTA pages at the top. More realistic targets are specific searches — by location, trip type or facility — and Google Maps rankings for local searches, which lead to bookings more reliably.

### Do we need to publish blog posts every week?

No. For hotels, the most important pages are room pages, location and getting-here pages, and FAQs. Complete those first, then add articles that answer real guest questions, such as transfers from the airport. One or two strong articles a month beat many thin ones.

### Does hotel SEO help AI tools recommend our hotel?

Indirectly, yes. AI tools such as Google's AI Mode draw on search results, Google Maps, reviews and websites. When your site gives complete, clear information that matches your Business Profile and OTA listings, AI is more likely to describe your hotel correctly — but nobody can guarantee an AI will recommend a particular hotel.

### What should a hotel SEO agency deliver, and what should we avoid?

A good service starts by auditing your website and Business Profile, maps keywords to existing pages, improves content and technical issues, and reports clicks, bookings and revenue from Search Console and analytics. Avoid anyone who guarantees the number-one spot, buys links in bulk, or reports only rankings without bookings.

## Summary

Effective hotel SEO starts with targets you can win — your name, specific searches and local searches — then fixes technical basics, builds complete room, location and FAQ pages, manages Google Maps alongside the site, and measures bookings. With frequent Google updates and AI starting to book on users' behalf, a website with accurate, complete information is the safest investment.
