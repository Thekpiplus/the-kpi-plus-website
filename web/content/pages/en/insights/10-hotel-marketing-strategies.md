# 10 Hotel Marketing Strategies: How to Plan for Bookings That Pay Off (Late 2026 Update)

**Hotel marketing strategies** are the decisions about who your hotel sells to, what experience it offers, how it prices and through which channels it sells — then using booking and cost data to judge the result. Good strategy doesn't mean doing every activity; it means choosing the ones that solve your real problem and measuring them through to revenue.

This guide collects ten decisions owners and teams should consider. **You don't need to invest in all ten at once.** Choose based on your problem, your resources and the dates you need to sell.

**Key points:** You don't need all ten at once · Start with goals and guests · Give each channel a clear role · Calculate promotions through to net revenue · Make the stay match the promise

## Late 2026: the context that changes strategy

Strategies that worked last year may need adjusting, because three developments change the assumptions behind hotel marketing plans in Thailand.

- **International demand isn't growing:** from 1 January to 19 September 2026, foreign arrivals were 3.67% lower than a year earlier (Ministry of Tourism and Sports). Strategies built on price-cutting to compete for the same guests risk lowering revenue for everyone.
- **Long-stay guests may change:** from 15 September 2026, the tourist visa exemption fell to a maximum of 30 days for 60 countries and territories (TAT). Hotels with long-stay packages should review terms and target guests.
- **Hotel search is moving into AI:** Google opened hotel booking in AI Mode to US users on 27 August 2026 (Skift). It hasn't reached Thailand yet, but complete, consistent hotel information on every channel matters more and more.

For the broader picture of how hotel marketing connects discovery to booking, see [what is hotel marketing (in Thai)](/insights/hotel-marketing).

## 1. Set marketing goals from your sales data

"More bookings" isn't specific enough to choose a plan. Break goals down by **stay date, room type, channel and rate** — for example, more room nights on Sunday to Thursday, more direct bookings, or selling a particular room type at a better rate.

![From 'more bookings' to a goal you can act on: stay dates Sunday–Thursday in November, Deluxe rooms with high availability, website direct bookings, a rate no lower than normal for the period, measured by room nights, ADR and cost per booking](/images/blog/10-hotel-marketing-strategies/en/goal-from-vague-to-clear.webp)

Check past data alongside what's already on the books for upcoming dates, and name the period you need to fix. Set measures before starting: room nights sold, ADR, revenue and cost per booking by channel.

**Example:** if Saturday already sells well but Sunday night is mostly empty, the goal might be to grow Sunday stays while holding Saturday's rate.

## 2. Understand your market and guests from real behaviour

Segment guests by why they travel and how they book, not just age or nationality. Families may care about room size and parking; business travellers about check-in time, location and Wi-Fi; long-stay guests about weekly or monthly rates and laundry.

![Segment guests by travel purpose, not just age or nationality: families value room size, extra beds, parking and pools; business travellers value check-in time, location and Wi-Fi; long-stay guests value weekly or monthly rates and quiet](/images/blog/10-hotel-marketing-strategies/en/guest-segments.webp)

Start with data you already have: which days guests stay, how far ahead they book, which rooms they choose, which channel they come from, and what they ask before booking. Read reviews and complaints alongside.

**Example:** what you should end up with — two or three core guest groups, why each should choose your hotel, and the dates you want to sell to each.

## 3. Position your brand around the experience you can deliver

A hotel brand isn't just a name, logo and colours; it's the expectations guests form from your photos, descriptions and the stay itself. A small hotel can stand out through a walkable location, family rooms, quiet, or staff who know the area.

Write selling points guests can verify — "walk to the restaurant district", with the real distance or walking time, or "a desk in room type X" — instead of broad claims like "the best" or "complete".

**Example:** a question to check your work — when guests arrive, does the real experience match what the listing said?

## 4. Choose sales channels and online marketing by the role each plays

OTAs such as Booking.com and Agoda reach people comparing stays. Your website serves people who search your name and want more detail. Google Business Profile shows your location, reviews and directions. Online ads push offers for specific dates. LINE Official Account answers pre-booking questions and keeps in touch with past guests.

![Each hotel channel has a different role: OTAs reach guests comparing stays, the hotel website takes direct bookings and gives full information, Google Business Profile shows location, reviews and directions, online ads push offers for need dates, and LINE answers pre-booking questions — with indicative cost per booking for each](/images/blog/10-hotel-marketing-strategies/en/channel-roles.webp)

Strategy isn't opening as many channels as possible; it's giving each a clear role. Check that rates, conditions, open inventory and room photos are correct everywhere, then look at sales after commission and costs by channel.

**Numbers to watch:** bookings, revenue, achieved rate and cost by channel and stay date

## 5. Make your website help guests decide and book

Your website should answer key questions easily on mobile: where you are, how rooms differ, what facilities you have, what the rates and conditions are, and how to book or get in touch.

![Test booking on your phone step by step: hotel page (location, real photos, a visible check-rates button), room page (how rooms differ and who they suit), dates and price (rates and cancellation clear), and confirmation (confirmation email and directions)](/images/blog/10-hotel-marketing-strategies/en/mobile-booking-path.webp)

Start with room pages, real photos, frequently asked details, directions and the booking flow. If you have online booking, test it on a phone through to confirmation, and check that rates and conditions match your other channels.

**Numbers to watch:** visitors who reach a room page, booking starts, completed bookings and where guests drop out of the booking flow

## 6. Use social media to build confidence before booking

Social media shows the atmosphere and how you care for guests, but you don't need every platform. Choose the ones your target guests use and your team can answer on time.

Plan content from pre-booking questions: which room suits families, whether there's parking, how to get from the airport, what's nearby. Use photos and videos that show reality.

**Numbers to watch:** quality enquiries, clicks to booking pages, reply time and trackable bookings. Likes alone say nothing about business results.

## 7. Create promotions and perks — and calculate whether they pay

A promotion should address the problem from strategy 1: filling weekdays, encouraging two-night stays, or selling a particular room type. Perks can be breakfast, late check-out or local experiences — as long as you know the cost.

Don't judge by extra bookings alone. The hypothetical example below shows bookings up 30%, but after commission and the cost of the perk, the hotel keeps less.

![Illustrative hotel promotion math: room nights up 30% and room revenue up 4%, but net revenue down 7.5% after 15% commission and breakfast cost of THB 150 per night](/images/blog/10-hotel-marketing-strategies/en/promotion-math.webp)

| Hypothetical example | Normal sales | Promotion −20% + breakfast |
|---|---|---|
| Room nights sold | 10 | 13 |
| Rate per night | THB 2,000 | THB 1,600 |
| Room revenue | THB 20,000 | THB 20,800 |
| 15% commission | THB 3,000 | THB 3,120 |
| Breakfast cost, THB 150/night | – | THB 1,950 |
| **Net revenue** | **THB 17,000** | **THB 15,730** |

Compare results with a similar period, and check whether the offer brought new guests or simply moved guests who'd have booked anyway to a lower price.

**Watch out:** before launching, define stay dates, eligible rooms, conditions, channels, number of rooms, cost and the date you'll review results.

## 8. Make the experience prove your marketing promises

Marketing brings guests in, but the stay proves whether the listing was true. A hotel that advertises easy access should send clear directions before arrival; one that promises a quiet stay must manage noise in practice.

Check the moments that most affect satisfaction: before arrival, check-in, cleanliness, answering questions and handling mistakes. Group recurring reviews and complaints and fix the root causes.

**Numbers to watch:** recurring review themes, time to resolve problems, complaints and the share of guests who return

## 9. Partner with local businesses to add value to the stay

Restaurants, cafés, activity operators and transfer providers help you offer experiences guests want without doing everything yourself — a curated local food guide, a package with an activity, or an airport transfer.

![Hotel local partners — you don't have to do everything yourself: restaurants and cafés, activities and tours, transfers, and spas and wellness, with clear agreements on price, responsibility and booking](/images/blog/10-hotel-marketing-strategies/en/local-partners.webp)

Choose partners for service quality, fit with your guests, and clarity on price, responsibility and booking. Once you work together, track whether guests actually use the service and what they say.

**Watch out:** check partners' details and readiness before you promise their service to guests.

## 10. Communicate with guests at the right time — and respect privacy

Before arrival, guests need booking confirmation, directions, check-in time and key conditions. During the stay, they need to know who to contact. Afterwards, they can be invited to share feedback and told how to book direct next time.

![Useful guest messages at the right time: before arrival (confirmation, directions, check-in and conditions), during the stay (who to contact, hotel services, fast problem-solving), after the stay (feedback request, review replies, how to book direct next time) — essential stay messages are sent as needed, marketing messages only with consent](/images/blog/10-hotel-marketing-strategies/en/guest-communication-journey.webp)

Make every message useful and avoid repetition. Email, LINE or direct messages should follow guests' preferences and personal data protection rules. Marketing messages need consent.

**Numbers to watch:** repeated questions, reply rate, pre-arrival problems, reviews and repeat bookings from guests who opted in

## Which hotel marketing strategies should a small hotel start with?

Start with **strategies 1 and 2** to know where sales need fixing and who your guests are, then check **strategies 4 and 5** for problems in channels or the booking flow. After that, choose one or two more based on the actual symptom.

![Where a small hotel should start with hotel marketing strategies: first know the brief (strategies 1–2), then check the booking path (4–5), then choose one or two of strategies 6–10 by symptom — many visitors but few bookings points to strategy 5 before more ads; empty weekdays point to strategies 2 and 7; recurring complaints point to strategy 8 before content](/images/blog/10-hotel-marketing-strategies/en/where-to-start.webp)

For example, if many people visit your website but few book, check room pages, rates, conditions and the booking flow before adding ad budget. If weekdays are empty but weekends sell, look at weekday guest behaviour and create an offer for those nights — without discounting the whole month.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many marketing strategies should a hotel have?

There's no fixed number. First set a clear goal, target guests and the numbers you'll measure, then choose the strategies your team can actually carry out. Doing two or three consistently and knowing the result beats starting ten and finishing none.

### What's the difference between a marketing strategy and a marketing tactic?

Strategy decides who you sell to, when, and with what value, price and channels. Tactics are the activities you use to get there — SEO, ads, content or promotions. Settle the strategy first, then pick the tactics that serve it.

### How do we know whether a strategy is working?

Define the result you want before starting, then check bookings, stay dates, achieved rates, revenue and cost by channel. Compare with a similar period and note outside factors such as holidays, events or price changes that may affect the numbers.

### What should hotels change in their marketing strategy for late 2026?

Three things: overall international demand isn't growing, so be careful with price-cutting; the tourist visa exemption is now 30 days, which may affect long-stay guests; and hotel search is starting to move into AI, so keep your information complete and consistent on every channel.

## Summary

These ten hotel marketing strategies are a framework for spending resources sensibly: start with goals and understanding guests, communicate strengths you can deliver, choose channels and offers by role and cost, and make the stay match the promise. In late 2026, with flat international demand, shorter visa-free stays and AI entering hotel search, doing a few things well and measuring them matters more than doing everything.
