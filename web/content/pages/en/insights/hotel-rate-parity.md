# Hotel Rate Parity: What It Means and How to Control Prices Across Channels (2026 Update)

**Hotel rate parity** means the same room type, on the same dates and terms, shows the same price on every sales channel — your website, the OTAs and Google. When prices differ (rate disparity), guests book wherever it's cheapest, which is rarely your own site, and you lose both commission and guest trust.

This guide is for owners, GMs and revenue teams selling through several OTAs. The question we answer: **why do rates leak, how do you check and fix them, and how do you win direct bookings without undercutting in ways your contracts may not allow?**

**Key points:** Parity means the same room on the same terms · Europe has banned parity clauses, but contracts for Thai hotels may still include them · Most leaks come from wholesalers and OTA-funded discounts · The price you send Google must match your booking page · Win direct bookings with perks rather than price cuts

## What changed: Europe dropped parity clauses, but pricing pressure remains

Parity rules in Europe have shifted since 2024. On 19 September 2024, the EU's top court said Booking.com's restrictions on hotels offering lower rates on their own websites or rival sites were unnecessary and could reduce competition, Reuters reported. From 14 November 2024, Booking.com has had to comply with the Digital Markets Act (DMA), which bans both wide and narrow parity clauses.

![Rate parity timeline in Europe: the EU court ruling in September 2024, Booking.com under the DMA from November 2024, and in September 2026 Booking.com no longer using outside prices to decide eligibility for Booking.com-sponsored discounts in the EEA](/images/blog/hotel-rate-parity/en/europe-timeline.webp)

Rental Scale-Up reported a further change in September 2026: Booking.com stopped using prices on other channels to decide whether EEA properties qualify for discounts it sponsors. Yet a Hospitality Net analysis this year argues that even without contract clauses, OTA ranking algorithms still reward properties whose prices are consistent.

**What this means for hotels:** these changes apply only to properties in Europe. Hotels in Thailand need to read their own OTA contracts — and whatever the contract says, consistent pricing still matters for OTA ranking, guest trust and how Google shows your rates.

## What is hotel rate parity, and how is it different from a parity clause?

Rate parity is a pricing approach; a parity clause is a contract term some OTAs have used to enforce it. There are two main types.

![Two types of parity clause compared: wide parity bans cheaper rates on any channel; narrow parity bans cheaper rates only on the hotel's own website, while other OTAs may be cheaper](/images/blog/hotel-rate-parity/en/wide-vs-narrow.webp)

| Type | Meaning | Status in Europe |
|---|---|---|
| Wide parity | No cheaper rate on any channel, including other OTAs and your website | Banned under the DMA |
| Narrow parity | No cheaper rate on your own website | Banned under the DMA |

**Watch out:** this article is general information, not legal advice. Contract terms vary by OTA and country — check your latest contracts before changing your pricing policy.

## Why do room rates differ between channels?

Most leaks aren't caused by the hotel setting the wrong rate, but by channels it doesn't directly control. Knowing the cause tells you where to fix it.

![Five causes of rate disparity: wholesale rates leaking to retail sites, OTA-funded discounts, member or mobile-app pricing, different tax and currency display, and manual update errors](/images/blog/hotel-rate-parity/en/disparity-causes.webp)

1. **Wholesale or bedbank leakage:** net rates given to tour operators get resold on public websites
2. **OTA-funded discounts:** the OTA cuts the price from its own commission, so guests see a lower rate even though you receive the same
3. **Member or app pricing:** discounts only for logged-in users or app bookings
4. **Tax and currency display:** one channel shows tax and service included, another shows pre-tax
5. **Manual updates:** a rate changed in one system but not another, when there's no channel manager

## 1. Check rates systematically

Effective checks compare the same room, dates, guest count and conditions — breakfast, cancellation policy. Otherwise you compare the wrong things and fix the wrong things.

![Four steps to check hotel rate parity: choose key dates, compare the same room and terms across channels, make a test booking to trace leaked rates, and fix at the source and record the result](/images/blog/hotel-rate-parity/en/parity-check-steps.webp)

| Check | Example |
|---|---|
| Dates | 7, 14 and 30 days ahead, long weekends, high season |
| Channels | Website, 2–3 main OTAs, Google and metasearch |
| Conditions | Room type, guests, breakfast, cancellation policy |
| Display | Tax and service included? Which currency? |

**Do this:** run a rate check every week, and log every case where prices differ by more than 3%, with the channel and date.

## 2. Close wholesale leaks

If you find a lower rate on a site you don't contract with directly, it usually comes from a wholesaler's net rate being resold. Make one real test booking and check which partner delivers it into your system — that tells you the source.

- State in contracts that net rates may not be sold room-only on public channels
- Separate package rates from room-only rates clearly
- Suspend allocation to partners who breach repeatedly

## 3. Talk to the OTA when its discounts undercut your website

When an OTA funds a discount from its commission, your revenue stays the same but guests see your website as more expensive. Check which discount programmes you've opted into, and ask your OTA account manager to explain where the lower price comes from.

**Numbers to watch:** % of dates where your website is more expensive than an OTA · leaks found per month · direct booking share

## 4. Win direct bookings with perks, not price cuts

The safest approach is to keep room rates equal and give guests another reason to book direct. This usually doesn't conflict with contracts and doesn't lower revenue per room.

![Direct-booking perks that don't cut the room rate: breakfast, early check-in or late check-out, an upgrade when available, and hotel credit](/images/blog/hotel-rate-parity/en/direct-perks.webp)

| Perk | Cost to hotel | Value to guest |
|---|---|---|
| Breakfast | Low to medium | High |
| Early check-in / late check-out (subject to availability) | Low | High |
| Upgrade when available | Low | High |
| Restaurant or spa credit | Medium (partly returns as revenue) | Medium |

Use the [OTA commission calculator (in Thai)](/tools/ota-commission-calculator) to see how much you save per direct booking, and size your perks accordingly.

## 5. Keep the price you send Google matching your booking page

If you use free booking links or Google hotel ads, the price you send to Google Hotel Center must match what guests see when they click into your booking engine. Google checks price accuracy continuously; if prices often don't match, it may show your price less or stop showing it.

**Do this:** check that the price shown on Google includes tax and service the same way your booking engine does, and recheck after every major rate change.

## A weekly hotel rate parity routine

Rate checks aren't a one-off task. Set a rhythm your team can keep and a clear owner.

![Hotel rate parity routine: every Monday check the next 30 days, after every rate change check immediately, monthly make real test bookings, and quarterly review OTA and wholesaler contracts](/images/blog/hotel-rate-parity/en/parity-routine.webp)

| When | Task | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly | Check the next 30 days and key dates | Revenue or reservations |
| After rate changes | Check all channels and Google | Whoever changed the rate |
| Monthly | Test-book on channels with unusually low rates | Revenue |
| Quarterly | Review OTA and wholesaler contracts | GM |

## Planning example: a 45-room hotel losing direct bookings

Imagine a 45-room hotel whose direct share falls from 22% to 15% over three months. A rate check finds that on 12 of the next 30 days, one OTA is 8–12% cheaper than the website — caused by a wholesaler's resold rate and an app-only OTA discount.

The team notifies partners and suspends allocation to the one in breach, matches the direct rate, adds free breakfast for direct bookings, and aims to bring the direct share back to 20% within two months. This is a planning example, not a result from any client.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a hotel in Thailand sell cheaper on its own website than on OTAs?

It depends on each OTA contract you have signed. The EU rules banning parity clauses apply to properties in the European Economic Area, so hotels in Thailand should read their own contracts. Many choose equal rates plus direct-booking perks — breakfast or late check-out — which usually doesn't conflict with contracts.

### What's the difference between rate parity and rate integrity?

Rate parity means the same room on the same terms has the same price on every channel. Rate integrity is broader: your whole rate structure makes sense — non-refundable below flexible, member rates with clear conditions, and no leaked rates from unauthorised channels. Hotels should manage both.

### Why are some OTAs showing our hotel cheaper than the rate we set?

Common causes are OTA-funded discounts, wholesale or bedbank rates resold without permission, app-only or member pricing, differences in how tax and currency are displayed, and manual update errors. Make a real test booking to see where the rate comes from.

### How does rate disparity affect Google?

Google Hotel Center checks the accuracy of the prices you send against the price on your booking page. If they often don't match, Google may show your price less or stop showing it in free booking links and hotel ads. Your booking-engine price must always match what you send to Google.

### How often should we check rate parity?

Check key dates at least weekly — the next 30 days, long weekends and high season — and immediately after changing rates or launching a promotion. Hotels with many channels can use an automated rate-shopping tool, but should still make a real test booking once a month to confirm.
